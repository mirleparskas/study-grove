# AHAD midterm: official instructor instructions

Re-supplied by the user October 18, 2026. Exam date: October 19, 2026; the announcement names October 19 and the year follows the current course context.

The midterm covers Modules 1-5. All content in the modules is testable, using all resources supplied for the course. There are 50 questions, all multiple choice. The exam is 90 minutes, 9:00-10:30 a.m. There are no AHAD long-answer or prescription-writing items.

For every common condition listed in the modules, review:

1. Assessment/history questions related to the presenting problem.
2. Basic and advanced health-assessment techniques to perform.
3. Diagnostic laboratory and other investigations to order, why, and how to interpret results.
4. Diagnosis, diagnostic criteria, and at least three differential diagnoses.
5. Whether consultation, referral or transfer of care is required.
6. Red flags.
7. Causative organisms, epidemiology and risk factors.
8. Application of the most current CNO Practice Standards.

Identify conditions the NP can diagnose/treat and conditions requiring consultation or care beyond the NP's scope. Responses should reflect what the NP should do in the actual clinical situation.

Read each question carefully, considering age, important words, abnormal findings, symptoms and allergies. Answer every item; there is no penalty for guessing. Eliminate obviously incorrect choices before selecting the best response to difficult questions.

The instructor recommends approximately one minute per MCQ when planning time. This is pacing advice, not a reduction of the 90-minute exam. Fifty questions in 90 minutes allows an overall average of 1.8 minutes (108 seconds) per item. A roughly 50-minute first pass would leave about 40 minutes for revisiting difficult items and checking answers; that two-pass plan is a study suggestion calculated from the stated times.

Module 5 includes Cardiology I, as previously confirmed by the user. Learning to produce three differentials is preparation for reasoning through MCQ options; it does not mean there is a written-response section.

These current instructor instructions take precedence over historical past-student descriptions of AHAD short/long answers or narrower topic predictions.
