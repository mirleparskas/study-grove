# Past-student topic notes and current exam format

Supplied by the user October 18, 2026. This is a historical student's recollection, not an official exam blueprint or verified answer key.

**Current clarification: AHAD is entirely multiple choice, with no long answers. THERA includes prescription writing like the case studies.** Earlier instructor THERA guidance includes approximately 10% short-answer/prescription items; these notes do not remove that guidance.

## Reported THERA priorities

- Approximately one item from each pressure/procedures/pharmacodynamics/delegation reading and approximately one from each case. Treat these counts as historical estimates.
- Patient modifiers: age, pregnancy/lactation, recent antibiotics, immune status, comorbidities and dermatitis. Identify urgent red flags and the required action before routine outpatient medication selection.
- MUMS primary medications, first-line choices and relevant alternatives; ages, contraindications, health teaching and follow-up. Past student recalls doses more prominently in scripts; the current instructor instructions still include adult/pediatric doses.
- Case-style prescriptions: pediatric pharyngitis, HEENT, AOM, pneumonia and amoxicillin-clavulanate. Concentration is supplied for a required pediatric suspension calculation.
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate: exact formulation and both components, reference lookup, three diarrhea/GI-tolerance reduction strategies.
- INCS: individual products and minimum approved ages, especially age two and under six.
- AOM: initial/alternative treatment, age and duration, recent antibiotic exposure, smoke risk, caregiver teaching. Confirm whether recalled dermatitis/dry-ear details actually concern OE or a nonintact/draining ear.
- OE: Polysporin, Sofracort and other appropriate choices in relation to TM integrity, inflammation, allergy and product contraindications. Do not learn a potentially ototoxic product as safe for a perforated or uncertain TM.
- Pharyngitis: pediatric dose and complete prescription; daycare/recent antibiotics are details to interpret using the actual diagnosis and reference, not a universal reason for broader treatment.
- Pneumonia: case-specific first-line/alternative therapy and script. The original pneumonia case has not been located in the current matched source set; added calculation exercises explicitly supply their regimen.
- Pregnancy/psoriasis: safe psoriasis treatments; recalled low-dose ASA should be checked as a separate obstetric prevention indication.
- COVID: early treatment risk/eligibility, treatment windows, drug interactions and organ function. Diabetes is a risk factor; it does not automatically define immunocompromised status.
- Necrotizing fasciitis: commonly taught types I/II, disproportionate pain/toxicity/rapid progression, emergency surgical assessment, IV antibiotics and debridement.
- Pertussis: macrolide treatment; erythromycin was recalled among options. Verify age, duration, contraindications, QT/interactions and the actual chosen reference.
- Compression: recalled CDRB pressure level, contraindications and vascular referral. The identified local venous-ulcer slide 8 describes a selected four-layer system at 30-40 mmHg; this does not establish the exact missing CDRB slide's recommendation for every patient.
- Delegation: legal restrictions, controlled acts, competence, responsibilities of delegator and recipient, no subdelegation.
- Reported written areas: Module 1 cases; HEENT case prescriptions; AOM caregiver education; pharyngitis; amoxicillin-clavulanate and diarrhea mitigation; anaphylaxis if part of the assigned cases. Recalled module numbers should be checked against this year's outline.

## AHAD topics to practise as MCQs

- AOM: S. pneumoniae, nontypeable H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis. The historical short-answer description does not apply to the current all-MCQ exam.
- Specific hypertension assessment: standardized repeat BP, appropriate arm comparison and orthostatics when indicated, heart rate/rhythm/auscultation, peripheral pulses, indicated carotid/abdominal/renal bruit assessment, HF/edema, fundoscopy, neurologic and secondary-cause/target-organ findings. Select clinically indicated elements rather than replacing them with the phrase “complete cardiac exam.”
- HEENT: cobblestoning in an allergic/postnasal-drip pattern; periorbital edema is nonspecific and eye pain/motility/visual red flags require escalation.
- Lesions, organisms, differentials, red flags and every supplied case from Modules 1-5, including Cardiology I in Module 5.

## Specific reconciliation of source conflicts

The seminar shorthand about smaller/more-frequent amoxicillin-clavulanate doses is not a universal rule. The 2026 Canadian Clavulin monograph compares its BID 200/400 schedules with its TID 125F/250F schedules and reports less diarrhea with the BID regimens. The CPS AOM guidance supplies a separate 7:1, TID regimen. Keep each source's indication, formulation, dose and frequency together.

Three conditional approaches included in study practice: limit unnecessary clavulanate with an appropriate formulation/regimen; give at the start of a meal; consider an evidence-supported probiotic in a suitable host. Severe/persistent/bloody diarrhea or dehydration needs assessment. Probiotics need special caution in susceptible/immunocompromised hosts.
